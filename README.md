# Empirical Evaluation of Bayesian Network Structure Learning Algorithms under Dimensional Scaling and Causal do-calculus

An MSc dissertation project evaluating constraint-based, score-based and hybrid Bayesian network structure-learning algorithms across benchmark network dimensions and sample sizes, with an additional assessment of downstream interventional fidelity.

## Project overview

Bayesian networks represent conditional dependencies between variables using directed acyclic graphs (DAGs). This project investigates how three structure-learning approaches behave as the number of variables and the available sample size increase, and whether structural recovery metrics alone provide a reliable indication of downstream interventional accuracy.

The study compares:

- **PC (Peter–Clark):** constraint-based structure learning using conditional-independence tests.
- **HC (Hill Climbing):** score-based greedy search using information criteria.
- **MMHC (Max–Min Hill Climbing):** a hybrid approach combining MMPC constraint-based candidate screening with score-based optimisation.

The evaluation uses three discrete benchmark networks:

| Benchmark | Variables (p) | Role in the study |
|---|---:|---|
| ALARM | 37 | Moderate-dimensional clinical monitoring network |
| Hailfinder | 56 | Intermediate-dimensional benchmark |
| Hepar II | 70 | Higher-dimensional liver-disorder network |

Each network is evaluated at five sample sizes: 500, 1,000, 2,500, 5,000 and 10,000 observations.

## Research questions

1. How do PC, HC and MMHC perform as sample size increases?
2. Does the relative performance of constraint-based and score-based learning change across network dimensions?
3. Which structural error mechanisms—missing edges, extra edges and orientation errors—explain algorithmic failure?
4. Do differences in global structural recovery translate into differences in local Markov blanket recovery and downstream interventional predictions?

## Methods

### Data generation and structure learning

Synthetic observational datasets are generated by ancestral sampling from fitted benchmark networks using `bnlearn::rbn()`. The supplied data-generation script uses fixed seeds for each benchmark network and generates the five sample-size tiers.

The pipeline evaluates baseline configurations and parameter sensitivity:

- PC: significance levels α = 0.01, 0.05 and 0.10.
- HC: AIC, BIC and eBIC scoring.
- MMHC: MMPC restriction with mutual-information testing, followed by BIC score maximisation.

### Evaluation layers

The R pipeline contains nine broad phases:

1. Baseline PC and HC runs on ALARM and Hepar II.
2. Hyperparameter sensitivity analysis for PC and HC.
3. MMPC skeleton screening and MMHC evaluation.
4. Three-network scaling analysis including Hailfinder.
5. Runtime profiling on ALARM and Hepar II.
6. Local Markov blanket precision, recall and F1-score for selected diagnostic variables.
7. Interventional simulation after graph mutilation, comparing downstream distributions using Total Variation Distance (TVD).
8. Structural error decomposition and Cirrhosis Markov blanket recall tracking.
9. Visualisation of structural convergence, error profiles, hyperparameter sensitivity, runtime, local recovery and interventional discrepancy.

For interventional evaluation, learned CPDAGs are extended to DAGs, local conditional probability tables are fitted using maximum likelihood estimation, the intervention target's incoming edges are removed and its state is fixed, and 50,000 observations are simulated. TVD is calculated as half the sum of absolute differences between the ground-truth and learned-network outcome distributions.

## Key findings reported in the dissertation

### 1. A dimensional crossover in structural performance

At 10,000 observations, PC achieved the lowest SHD on ALARM (p = 37), while HC performed best on the larger networks:

| Benchmark | PC SHD | HC SHD | MMHC SHD | Lowest SHD |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| ALARM (p = 37) | 12 | 30 | 19 | PC |
| Hailfinder (p = 56) | 41 | 16 | 41 | HC |
| Hepar II (p = 70) | 107 | 49 | 80 | HC |

The dissertation interprets these results as an empirical crossover around p ≈ 50 nodes, with the transition observed between the 37-node ALARM and 56-node Hailfinder benchmarks. This is a finding across the three selected networks, not a universal threshold established for all possible graph topologies.

### 2. Different algorithms exhibit different structural failure modes

At n = 10,000, the reported CPDAG-space SHD decomposition was:

| Benchmark | Algorithm | Missing (FN) | Extra (FP) | Reversed | Unoriented | SHD |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| ALARM | PC | 9 | 0 | 2 | 1 | 12 |
| ALARM | HC | 4 | 10 | 16 | 0 | 30 |
| ALARM | MMHC | 12 | 0 | 7 | 0 | 19 |
| Hepar II | PC | 73 | 4 | 23 | 7 | 107 |
| Hepar II | HC | 40 | 1 | 8 | 0 | 49 |
| Hepar II | MMHC | 65 | 0 | 15 | 0 | 80 |

The dissertation attributes PC's high-dimensional error primarily to missing edges under high-order conditioning, while HC's ALARM error includes extra edges and orientation errors. MMHC's Hepar II results are discussed in terms of an upstream constraint-filter bottleneck.

### 3. Local Markov blanket recovery depends on the target

At n = 10,000, all three methods achieved F1 = 1.000 for the ALARM BP target. For the dense Hepar II Cirrhosis target, the reported F1-scores were 0.438 for PC, 0.718 for HC and 0.438 for MMHC. This illustrates that global and local performance can differ by network and target-node density.

### 4. Global SHD does not determine interventional fidelity

The reported TVD values at n = 10,000, using 50,000 Monte Carlo draws, include:

| Intervention | Outcome | PC TVD | HC TVD | MMHC TVD |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|
| ALARM: do(HYPOVOLEMIA = first factor level) | CVP | 0.0025 | 0.1135 | 0.1158 |
| ALARM: do(HYPOVOLEMIA = first factor level) | HR | 0.0021 | 0.0010 | 0.0060 |
| Hepar II: do(Cirrhosis = first factor level) | jaundice | 0.0003 | 0.0051 | 0.0025 |

The dissertation describes two contrasting mechanisms: localised causal shielding, where errors outside the active causal pathway have limited impact on the target outcome; and parameter-partitioning distortion, where spurious parent links alter fitted conditional probability tables.

**Intervention implementation note:** The supplied implementation selects the first factor level for each intervention target using `levels(...)[1]`. The dissertation reports these levels as `FALSE` for HYPOVOLEMIA and `absent` for Cirrhosis. Verify factor-level ordering in the generated datasets before reproducing the intervention experiments.

## How to run

1. Install the required R packages:

   ```r
   install.packages(c("bnlearn", "ggplot2"))
   ```

2. Place the fitted benchmark network RDS files in the project root:

   - `alarm.rds`
   - `hailfinder.rds`
   - `hepar2.rds`

   The RDS files must contain fitted `bnlearn` networks accepted by `bnlearn::rbn()`.

3. Generate the synthetic datasets:

   ```bash
   Rscript R/data_generation.R
   ```

4. Run the analysis pipeline:

   ```bash
   Rscript R/pipeline_execution.R
   ```

The pipeline expects the generated CSV files in `data/` and the three benchmark RDS files in the project root. It prints result tables and creates plots in the active R graphics device. The current pipeline displays plots through the active R graphics device and prints result tables. It does not automatically export all figures and tables to files. Additional `ggsave()` and `write.csv()` calls can be added to automate output generation.

## Reproducibility and interpretation notes

- The data-generation script sets one seed per benchmark network. The study's structural results therefore represent one generated dataset per network/sample-size combination, rather than repeated-seed averages.
- The interventional simulations use 50,000 Monte Carlo observations and set seed 42 before each network's simulation block.
- Runtime measurements are hardware- and environment-dependent; the dissertation's reported times should be interpreted as measurements from its original execution environment, not as universal benchmarks.
- The crossover interpretation is based on three benchmark networks (p = 37, 56 and 70). The dissertation identifies finer dimensional sweeps and repeated simulation seeds as future work.
- The intervention analysis covers selected intervention–outcome pairs, not every possible intervention in each network.
- The plotting code's longitudinal structural-error breakdown includes PC and HC; MMHC is evaluated elsewhere in the pipeline and is included in the reported baseline comparisons, but is not included in those two stacked error-breakdown plots.

## Limitations and future work

The dissertation identifies several limitations and extensions:

- Repeat each simulation across multiple seeds and report uncertainty intervals.
- Evaluate more benchmark topologies around the proposed dimensional crossover region.
- Expand interventional testing to additional intervention–outcome pairs.
- Sample multiple valid DAG extensions from learned CPDAG equivalence classes to quantify TVD sensitivity to orientation choices.
- Extend the framework to latent confounding and dynamic Bayesian networks for time-dependent clinical data.
